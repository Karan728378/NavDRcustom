### 1.3 Active architecture versus retained legacy code

The selected runtime path is:

**Simulation, browser motion input, or imported recording → Workbench → NavigationSession → fixed-mount alignment → planar EKF → optional outage HMM → selected position → map and evaluator.**

The TCN processes IMU samples in the session, but its velocity correction is disabled by default in the real HTML checkbox. Enabling it permits the untrained speed observation during qualifying outages. A replay ablation separately evaluates the TCN/anchor branch without the EKF position output.

Several older modules are still loaded and initialized, but their update methods do not drive the selected navigation loop. These include the older drift generator in `engine.js`, `DeadReckoningEngine`, weighted `SensorFusion`, heuristic `AIDriftCorrection`, nearest-segment `MapMatcher`, and `HandheldCompensation`. Some retain scripted drift, fabricated confidence values, or reference-dependent interfaces. Their existence does not prove those defects still drive the main benchmark. Equally, their existence does not establish working phone-handling protection or learned correction in the active system.

This distinction matters for migration: use the current session implementation and its tests as the starting specification. Blindly porting files with promising names would reintroduce obsolete behavior. Existing README/implementation passages describing the old fixed-regression motion baseline are also stale: the current estimator has replaced that path with the untrained TCN.

### 1.4 What the implemented mathematics actually provides

**TCN:** six IMU channels, a 32-channel projection, eight kernel-size-six convolutions with dilations `[1,1,2,2,4,4,16,16]`, residual connections, and a scalar head. Network computation happens on each incoming sample; the exposed speed updates approximately every 50 ms. The reported processing time is local JavaScript work for that sample, not Android inference latency or total sensor-to-position latency. Confidence is explicitly uncalibrated. No supervised learning, quantization, checkpoint loading, or mobile-runtime conversion is present.

The 231-sample receptive field represents about 2.3 seconds at 100 Hz. IO-VNBD's smartphone collection is described as 10 Hz IMU with 1 Hz phone GPS. At 10 Hz, the same sample count spans about 23 seconds, and the current estimator cannot attain its expected 90–110 Hz ready status. This is a dataset/model contract mismatch that must be resolved before training. Upsampling cannot recreate the missing high-frequency signal. [IO-VNBD paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.01701)

**EKF:** covariance propagation, Joseph-form measurement updates, a two-dimensional GNSS innovation gate, and separate speed/course updates are implemented. A moving GNSS course above 2 m/s initializes heading. This is legitimate filtering code. Its noise values are engineering defaults, not measured phone specifications. Fixed gravity subtraction assumes an unchanged mounting orientation and approximately level travel. Speed clipping, forward-only motion, and the lack of full attitude dynamics limit ramps, reverse motion, banked turns, and changing mounts.

The displayed 95% ring comes from the horizontal EKF covariance before map matching. It is not calibrated empirical coverage of the selected HMM-projected position. A numerically positive-definite covariance is necessary but does not establish that 95% of real errors fall inside the ring.

**HMM:** the supplied OSM import yields 2,692 nodes and 4,411 directed edges. The matcher uses up to eight candidates, approximately 2 Hz observations, bounded path history, and shortest-path searches capped at 2 km. Graph size is capped at 5,000 nodes/12,000 edges. It does not implement complete vehicle access rules, turn restrictions, closures, lane or floor discrimination, or country-scale map management. Its result is a road constraint, not a complete routing system.

The current synthetic route was generated from the same OSM snapshot used for matching. OSM is real external geometry, but the scenario is deliberately constructed to follow it. This is useful integration testing; it is not an independently measured vehicle trajectory or evidence of unseen-route generalization. The alternative scenario graph shares the trajectory even more directly and is labeled accordingly.

**NavIC and signal quality:** the Android recorder can observe IRNSS satellite status, and `gnss-quality.js` uses a short C/N₀ trend to inflate GNSS measurement variance. This is a heuristic quality input, not raw NavIC measurement fusion or a demonstrated proactive NavIC-to-inertial handoff. No working barometer integration or learned phone-handling compensation was found in the active navigation pipeline. Satellite visibility/status must not be presented as proof of NavIC-derived position accuracy. [Android GnssStatus](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/location/GnssStatus)

### 1.6 Remaining correctness and evidence problems

1. **Outage pooling can conceal failure.** The core divides the maximum error across outages by the sum of distance across outages. Two separate 100 m outages with a 15 m maximum error would produce 7.5% under that aggregation even though an individual outage reached 15%. Separate scenario runs reset state, so this chiefly affects multi-outage recordings/manual runs. Report each outage independently and keep any aggregate secondary.
2. **Null metrics can become numeric zero in the scenario export layer.** `advance()` uses `m.driftPercent || 0` and `m.improvementPercent || 0`. This can write 0% alongside an insufficient/incomplete status. The status may prevent a formal pass, but the number is misleading. Replayed/device frames do not use the simulation's benchmark-advance path, so exporting current replay/device metrics through this state can be stale or unpopulated. Export from the actual immutable result.
3. **Raw and non-EKF branches accept GNSS that the EKF rejects.** The raw integrator resets on each supplied fix before the filter's gating decision. Rejected fixes cannot re-anchor the TCN itself, but can still corrupt raw/estimated positions and their comparison. Either apply comparable quality rules or explicitly evaluate different GNSS policies as separate ablations.
4. **Signal-quality down-weighting does not fully govern the selected output.** C/N₀ degradation increases EKF measurement variance, but a fresh accepted fix is selected directly for position while GNSS is available. That bypasses the filter's weighted position at those instants. A native implementation needs an explicit output and recovery policy.
5. **Synthetic conditions are unusually favorable.** The active generator supplies exact healthy GNSS with nominal accuracy 3 m, simple bounded IMU noise, and a fixed mount. It does not establish performance under measured bias instability, temperature drift, delayed fixes, bad initialization, or phone movement. Piecewise route turns and button-driven speed changes also do not constitute realistic vehicle dynamics.
6. **Reference validity and stationary behavior need a benchmark specification.** No uncertainty model or independent timestamp accompanies reference positions. Repeated noisy GNSS points can inflate path length. Very short/stationary intervals make normalized drift unstable. Initialization failures and missing-output periods must be counted rather than disappearing from a success denominator.
7. **Warm-up and rate behavior need explicit fallbacks.** The model's arbitrary GNSS anchor has no validated lifetime; the current code exposes anchor age but does not establish trustworthy behavior over long outages. The network may also consume CPU even when its position contribution is disabled.
8. **Reacquisition and map-state reset need broader coverage.** The HMM is updated only during outages and retains prior state between them. Recovery, long gaps between outages, parallel roads, and rejected recovery fixes need dedicated sequence tests and field validation.

These are source-review findings, not newly implemented fixes. Existing passing tests do not cover all of them.

### 1.8 The smartphone migration gap

The active browser input uses `DeviceMotionEvent` and browser geolocation, with a fixed flat-phone calibration. It cannot expose Android `GnssStatus`, hardware FIFO configuration, native sensor timestamps/control, or Android service lifecycle through those APIs. No installed PWA manifest/service worker or native sensor bridge was found. Installing the same page as a PWA would not grant those missing interfaces.

The [Android recorder](/home/rimuru/Downloads/Antigravity/SIH_Pototype/native/android/app/src/main/java/org/navdr/recorder/MainActivity.java) is useful scaffolding: Java 17 source, API 30 minimum, SDK 35 configuration, requested 100 Hz accelerometer/gyro, GPS fixes, satellite C/N₀ and IRNSS observations, and JSON export. Native sample timestamps are preserved. However:

- It has no magnetometer or `TYPE_ROTATION_VECTOR` acquisition, navigation filter, learned inference runtime, map display, or live bridge to the browser.
- It stops recording in `onPause`. A foreground Activity is not a foreground Service.
- It does not configure batching latency/FIFO capability, a dedicated ingestion thread, or long-running navigation lifecycle.
- It stores the full recording in memory and stops at 200,000 samples: approximately 33 minutes at 100 Hz. That is incompatible with multi-hour operation.
- It attaches satellite observations to frames containing a fresh GNSS fix. Satellite changes during an actual loss of fixes may therefore never reach replay. The monitoring path needs its own timestamped event stream.
- Gyro/accelerometer pairing is a basic latest-sample check, not a validated synchronization/resampling pipeline.

Native Android supports the required acquisition and service mechanisms, but capability varies by phone and must be measured. Batching trades delivery delay against power use; a foreground service alone does not prove sustained sampling. [Android sensors overview](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/sensors/sensors_overview), [motion sensors](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/sensors/sensors_motion), [sensor batching](https://source.android.com/docs/core/interaction/sensors/batching?hl=en), [foreground service types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types)

**Gap estimate:** with six full-time contributors covering the required skills, allow roughly 4–6 elapsed weeks for a narrow native acquisition/navigation/inference vertical slice, and 12–16 weeks for a defensible SIH package, with a further 4-week contingency for data, training, and field failures. These are planning estimates, not established velocity or an accuracy guarantee. Market readiness is a separate multi-month effort.

A “PWA with native bridging” is effectively a native host plus a web interface. It is not a shortcut around learning Android. If team coverage turns out to be theoretical rather than hands-on, reserve a 2–4 week learning/integration allowance and obtain experienced review; do not hide that cost inside “just use a bridge.”

**Proposed boundary:** sensors, synchronization, alignment, fusion, model inference, outage state, logging, and output integrity belong to the native core. Rendering and experiment inspection can remain in the browser. A native core need not immediately become cross-platform C++; Kotlin is a reasonable first Android implementation. Consider a portable core only when an iOS/other-platform buyer justifies its cost.

## 2. Track A: SIH-ready application and evidence

### 2.1 Objective and team ownership

Track A ends with a reproducible preliminary-model submission package and a smartphone application that demonstrates the requested pipeline on real data. Its scope is prototype quality within a declared operating envelope. It does not include a nationwide consumer navigation service, universal phone compatibility, or commercial reliability commitments.

Proposed initial envelope: Android, fixed supported mounts, passenger cars and light vans, mapped roads, and explicitly tested outage durations. The full requirement checklist must still cover automated alignment and phone disturbance handling if required by the organizer; a fixed-mount milestone is not the final substitute for those requirements. Arbitrary handheld operation, multi-level parking, and multi-hour accurate inertial navigation are separate research challenges.

### 2.5 Real training and deployment pipeline

**Data and labels.** Start with the IO-VNBD smartphone IMU stream, not vehicle CAN signals disguised as phone inputs. The official repository provides synchronized and unsynchronized collections; only verified corresponding trips can support paired supervision. Where pairing exists, use vehicle speed measurements as labels after checking units, slip, quality, time offset and sensor lever arms. Wheel angular speed requires a defensible conversion to linear speed; do not assume its units are m/s. Phone-GNSS-derived speed may support a separately labeled weak-supervision experiment on good reception, but is not precision truth. [Official IO-VNBD repository](https://github.com/onyekpeu/IO-VNBD)

A vehicle's CAN data can be used for training/reference without becoming a runtime dependency. The deployed smartphone model must continue to operate without CAN, wheel speed, a reference receiver or evaluator truth. Record this distinction in the model card.

**Rate contract.** For the first model, train a causal model appropriate to the genuine 10 Hz IO-VNBD input and a declared window duration. Native fusion may still use 100 Hz acquisition while the model receives an anti-aliased 10 Hz stream. Include downsampling delay in the latency budget. This is preferable to pretending the dataset contains 100 Hz motion. Develop a separate 100 Hz model only after collecting adequate real 100 Hz training data; validate any transfer rather than assuming it works. Version the sampling/filtering contract with the model.

**Learning task.** Compare scalar forward-speed regression with an anchored velocity-change formulation that receives only the last genuinely accepted GNSS initialization and its age. Declare the anchor explicitly if used. Neither formulation may consume future windows, masked GNSS values, map truth, or evaluation positions. Constant velocity cannot in general be inferred uniquely from an ideal IMU alone, so learned vibration correlations and initial velocity are assumptions to test, not universal guarantees.

**Splits.** Partition whole trips before generating overlapping windows. Hold out vehicle identity, environment/route groups, phone model and mounting configurations where metadata permits. Use grouped cross-validation, preferably leave-one-vehicle-out where enough distinct vehicles exist, with an inner development split for tuning. Keep the corresponding smartphone and vehicle records in the same fold. Fit normalizers and INT8 calibration data only on training/development material allowed by the protocol. Prevent near-duplicate trips and overlapping windows from crossing splits.

Some IO-VNBD paired data may not span the desired vehicle/phone matrix. Do not invent folds to claim that it does. Report supported leave-group-out evaluations and fill missing generalization categories with newly collected local data. Keep the final Indian-road corpus unseen through model selection. Confidence intervals should resample trips/vehicles, not treat thousands of correlated IMU windows as independent trials.

**Training evidence.** Store dataset checksums, split manifests, preprocessing settings, seeds, optimizer/loss settings, epoch logs, selected checkpoint, and all baseline results. Validate basic units/signs and overfit a tiny development subset to expose pipeline errors, then assess generalization. Use velocity MAE/RMSE and regime-specific bias, but select the integrated model using outage trajectory results as well. A lower speed MAE can still produce worse integrated position.

**Model-to-filter interface.** Specify the observation timestamp, any effective delay, speed units, valid range, uncertainty/quality, warm-up state and unsupported-input handling. Estimate measurement-noise settings on development residuals. Overlapping model windows and the IMU-driven filter have correlated errors; blindly treating every learned speed as an independent, high-confidence measurement can make the filter overconfident. Use conservative rates/noise and empirical coverage checks before attempting a more elaborate statistical treatment.

**Attitude inputs.** Record magnetometer and rotation-vector streams where supported, but establish what information the chosen Android sensor implementation already fuses before combining it with raw gyro/accelerometer updates. Avoid double-counting correlated attitude information. Test magnetic disturbances inside vehicles and reject unreliable magnetic heading; capture support is not proof of reliable heading or arbitrary-mount alignment.

**Runtime choice.** Prefer a small CPU-first LiteRT deployment if conversion/operator support and device measurements are satisfactory. A PyTorch model can be converted with LiteRT Torch; prove compatibility early with the intended convolutions, state/window representation and normalization. ONNX Runtime Mobile is a legitimate alternative if it provides a simpler verified export path. Pin one runtime after the Week 4 export experiment rather than carrying both indefinitely. [LiteRT Torch](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/litert-torch/blob/main/README.md), [ONNX Runtime Mobile](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/tutorials/mobile/)

**INT8 deployment.** Export FP32 first and establish desktop-to-device numerical parity. Then apply supported static INT8 quantization using representative training/development windows covering stationary, acceleration, turning, vibration, and sensor ranges. Inspect which operations actually run as integers; weight-only or mixed-float execution must be labeled accurately. If post-training quantization harms accuracy, try quantization-aware training or retain FP32 pending improvement. INT8 is an optimization candidate, not evidence of correctness or automatic energy savings. [AI Edge Quantizer](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/ai-edge-quantizer)

Compare FP32 and INT8 on the same frozen development sequences at both velocity and final trajectory levels. Proposed initial tolerance: no more than 0.5 percentage-point increase in the registered drift summary, no unexplained new failure category, and device speed-output differences within a pre-agreed engineering tolerance. Set exact thresholds before examining final test outcomes. Record model size, total runtime/APK size, startup cost, sustained latency and energy; parameter count alone cannot predict these.

If the authenticated PS mandates INT8 specifically, an FP32 fallback leaves that requirement open; report it and allocate further work instead of declaring the gate satisfied.

Do not build the new runtime strategy around NNAPI: it is deprecated starting with Android 15. CPU profiling is a valid starting point for this small model. [Android NNAPI status](https://source.android.com/docs/core/interaction/neural-networks)

### 2.6 Benchmark methodology and claim rules

**Three evidence levels must remain separate.**

| Evidence | What it supports | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Synthetic IMU/reference | Software regression, edge-case injection, deterministic debugging | Real phone accuracy or trained-model generalization |
| Real recorded sensors with software-masked estimator GNSS | Real-data outage reconstruction against a withheld reference; preliminary IO-VNBD position plots | A physical tunnel trial by itself; independence/precision if the only reference is the same phone GPS |
| Real physical GNSS degradation/outage with an independent reference | Field behavior for the tested hardware, routes and conditions | Universal phone/vehicle performance or accuracy outside reference coverage |

For software masking, retain reference data only in the evaluator and remove masked GNSS position, speed, course and derived corrections from every runtime input. Clearly disclose any deliberate use of satellite quality during a masked experiment. Do not disable the reference recorder to create the outage. Removing/altering the reference field must leave the estimated trajectory unchanged.

For true tunnels, an ordinary second GNSS receiver may also lose reception. Obtain a reference that remains accurate for that trial, such as a surveyed controlled course or a suitably validated reference INS/odometry system with a known error budget. RTK alone does not solve a satellite-denied tunnel. Borrow/rent reference equipment or collaborate with a lab if necessary; this is test instrumentation, not dedicated hardware required by the product. If adequate reference is unavailable, show the physical demo qualitatively and keep quantitative claims attached to qualified masked real-data trials.

For each outage j, define distance **D_j** as the reference path length within that outage and **E_peak,j** as the maximum horizontal selected-output error within it. Report **100 × E_peak,j / D_j**, alongside endpoint error, RMSE, along-track/cross-track error, outage duration, and initialization error. Use the organizer's exact primary metric when authenticated; retain these diagnostic metrics even if its definition differs. Do not divide error from one outage by distance from other outages.

Use registered masks spanning 5, 10, 30 and 60 seconds, then 120 seconds and longer stress cases where reference and routes permit. Include distance-based cases such as 50 m and 1 km if required by the official scoring contract. Stratify by turns, stop/start traffic, rough roads, low speed, initial GNSS quality and recovery. Stationary/near-zero-distance cases report absolute error and drift rate, not a misleading normalized percentage.

Reference error should be materially smaller than the allowed error; a proposed design target is reference uncertainty below 20% of the error allowance. If the reference cannot distinguish a pass from a fail, label the result inconclusive. Timestamp uncertainty, lever arms and path-length estimation belong in that error budget. Do not smooth a trajectory against NavDR's own result or snap truth to the same road graph to manufacture agreement.

Run independent fixed-configuration sessions on identical inputs:

1. Last valid position and constant-speed/heading extrapolation baselines.
2. The reviewed planar raw integrator, with its constraints disclosed.
3. Classical filter without learned velocity or map projection.
4. Trained velocity + filter without map projection.
5. Classical filter + HMM without trained velocity.
6. Trained velocity + filter + HMM.

Retain the untrained network only as a labeled negative control. If applicable, compare 15-state versus planar fusion separately. For GNSS gating, either share the same accepted input policy or label a separate experiment that measures the gating advantage. For market comparisons, add the partner's incumbent provider and mapping stack under equivalent conditions; do not assume the raw integrator represents Google, Apple or MapmyIndia.

Proposed final evaluation minimum: at least 30 independent trips across the declared supported test matrix, with all registered outages retained. This is an engineering starting point, not a statistically sufficient universal accuracy certification. Report the number of trips, vehicles, phones and outage cases, the fraction below target, per-stratum median/p95/worst error, and cluster-based uncertainty. Count no-output, failed initialization, off-network, sensor-gap and invalid-reference outcomes separately. Any headline pass must state the tested envelope and actual criterion. “All valid registered cases below 10%” is stronger than “median below 10%”; do not interchange them.

For SIH app performance, proposed initial targets are nominal 100 Hz native acquisition on supported phones, no unexplained sample loss, p95 sensor-to-position age below 100 ms, and UI updates at 5–10 Hz. Measure worst sustained behavior as well as averages. A GNSS receiver usually reveals loss after some delay; distinguish inference/output continuity from the time needed to decide that a fix is stale. Do not advertise millisecond physical outage detection based only on a fast software switch.

### 2.7 Submission and finale artifacts

The preliminary package contains the actual model/checkpoint, inference entry point, exact IO-VNBD subset IDs and split manifest, preprocessing/label definitions, baseline comparisons, position-overlay and error plots, measured results including failures, a short model card, and reproduction instructions. Supply the organizer's required file formats and model packaging once verified.

A fallback replay uses recorded real data and is explicitly labeled as replay. Screenshots and a video are supplementary evidence; the executable and original logs must be inspectable. Preserve failed runs in the report rather than allowing a polished presentation to erase them.

## 3. Track B: market-fit SDK and a viable business

### 3.1 Commercial starting point and competition

**No actual first paying customer has been identified in the supplied evidence.** There is no demonstrated paid pilot, signed commercial commitment, procurement owner, price acceptance, or production integration. Naming a company as “the first customer” now would invent evidence. The plan below specifies how to obtain one and what must be true before proceeding.

Google Maps/Android, Apple, and MapmyIndia are established navigation and positioning competitors with substantial accumulated engineering and distribution advantages. This is an occupied market:

- Google has documented urban positioning research combining building geometry, raw satellite measurements and machine learning, with improvements delivered through its location platform. That is evidence of an incumbent capability base, not a directly comparable result for NavDR's phone-only tunnel experiment. [Google's urban GPS work](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2020/12/improving-urban-gps-accuracy-for-your.html)
- Apple Core Location combines multiple device components and provides location/orientation services to app developers. It is already part of the location stack a future iOS customer would use. [Apple Core Location](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation?changes=l_3)
- MapmyIndia sells navigation software/SDKs and explicitly describes integration with in-vehicle dead reckoning. It is therefore both an incumbent navigation supplier and a potential alternative to purchasing a small specialist SDK. [MapmyIndia navigation](https://www.mapmyindia.com/navigation/)

These sources do not establish the precise R&D expenditure or hidden algorithms of each company, or prove that each solves NavDR's exact operating conditions. They do establish that “navigation stops when GPS fails, and nobody solves it” is an indefensible pitch. B2B licensing avoids some consumer distribution problems, but it does not remove incumbent competition.

The credible initial hypothesis is a **location-continuity SDK for Android fleet applications that lack in-house navigation expertise**, improving a measurable weakness in their existing provider. The partner keeps its maps, routing, dispatch and customer relationship. NavDR supplies position continuity, validity/uncertainty and diagnostic evidence within a bounded envelope.

### 3.2 First paying customer hypothesis

Target a regional logistics software vendor or a fleet operator with its own Android driver application, approximately 200–2,000 active vans/cars, repeated routes through signal-obstructed areas, and an engineering team able to integrate an SDK. The first deployment should use mounted phones and a known set of models. A van fleet is a closer fit to the proposed SIH envelope than delivery riders keeping phones in pockets.

The economic buyer is its product/engineering head or operations leader with a location-related budget. The engineering sponsor controls the driver's location pipeline. The operational pain must be demonstrable: wrong-road assignment, lost trace continuity, incorrect arrival/geofence events, avoidable support calls, or dispatch uncertainty. A business that merely likes an animated map is not a qualified buyer.

Before calling anyone the first customer, record the company's name, budget owner, affected workflow, current location/map provider, measured issue frequency and cost, supported fleet/phone mix, access to evaluation data, integration sponsor, and willingness to sign a paid pilot with a conversion decision. The team must supply introductions or approve subsequent outreach. No outreach was performed in this planning session.

Large ride-hailing platforms may be later customers, partners or competitors, but they can have substantial internal capability, demanding reliability requirements and long procurement cycles. Do not assume they will be the easiest first buyer. Emergency navigation and autonomous driving are outside the initial commercial claim.

### 3.4 A concrete first-pilot design

Propose one city/region, one partner application, 20–50 vehicles, 3–5 supported phone models, and a 4–6 week operational observation window within the broader pilot phase. Start with mounted phones and declared car/van classes. Agree the sampling plan before collecting results, including how many genuinely affected trips are needed; hundreds of ordinary open-sky trips cannot substitute for relevant outage exposure.

**Stage 1: baseline and shadow mode.** For 1–2 weeks, let the current provider drive operations while NavDR calculates a parallel output. Log enough timing, validity and error information to compare them. Use an independently referenced subset for accuracy; partner labels such as “delivered” are useful operational outcomes, not precise position truth. Confirm there is no unacceptable battery, app-stability or privacy regression.

**Stage 2: controlled operational use.** After shadow-mode acceptance, enable a small cohort using a kill switch and explicit fallback to the partner provider. Compare by vehicle/day or another suitable cluster, controlling for route, handset, traffic and operating period. Avoid comparing NavDR on easy routes with the incumbent on difficult ones. Do not immediately feed experimental estimates into billing, punitive driver decisions or high-consequence dispatch logic.

**Stage 3: joint review and purchase decision.** Agree thresholds before deployment. A possible negotiation starting point is a 20% relative reduction in a clearly defined location-related failure rate, no worsening of unacceptable wrong-road assignments, and an incremental energy/stability budget the partner approves. These are proposed business targets, not measured NavDR performance. A baseline with almost no failures may make a pilot uneconomic regardless of technical novelty.

Also report absolute numbers: affected trips per week, seconds of unusable positioning, meters of error where reference exists, extra battery percentage points per hour under matched conditions, support events, and integration hours. “<10% drift” can still mean nearly 100 m error over 1 km, which may be unacceptable for arrival/geofence logic. Commercial acceptance needs absolute and workflow-specific limits.

The paid pilot should cover integration, field support, reference instrumentation and evaluation. Before the pilot starts, agree recurring pricing mechanics, the scale-up decision date and who funds ongoing support. Avoid indefinite free customization that produces a demo but no repeatable product. A pilot ends with rollout, a narrowly scoped remediation period, or a stop decision.

### 3.5 What a partner engineering team needs from the SDK

Ship a versioned Android AAR/Maven artifact, a minimal Kotlin/Java host example, supported Android/ABI/device requirements, a permissions and service integration guide, and a reproducible replay example. Target an engineer running the sample in one day and achieving a staging integration in roughly 3–5 engineer-days. Those are product targets to test with someone outside the NavDR team.

The contract must state who owns GNSS and sensor collection. Prefer one owner per stream so the SDK and host app do not accidentally duplicate high-power subscriptions. Support either NavDR-managed collection or an explicit timestamped-input adapter, with clearly documented units and frames. Returning a NavDR position must not silently replace the OS location provider or require mock-location privileges.

Output needs position, speed, heading, monotonic measurement time, age, coordinate frame, active source/mode, validity, uncertainty semantics, and a reason for degraded/unavailable output. Distinguish raw accepted GNSS, filter output, and road-constrained output so a partner can choose appropriately. Allow evaluation export without coupling the SDK to the NavDR console.

Lifecycle behavior must cover session start/stop, app backgrounding, foreground-service ownership, permission revocation, missing sensors, GNSS recovery, model failure and process restart. Bound queues and storage. Offline licensing/model availability must not abruptly interrupt navigation because a server is unreachable. The SDK also needs release notes, migration guidance, deterministic regression recordings, a support escalation path, model/map version compatibility, and rollback.

The partner keeps routing and maps unless the pilot explicitly includes NavDR graph packaging. Check that its map/navigation license permits use of an external location estimate and any required data processing. Do not assume a partner SDK accepts arbitrary position injection merely because it draws a map.

### 3.6 Production engineering beyond the SIH build

**Generalization:** expand a structured matrix of sensor vendors, phone price/performance classes, OS versions, mount positions, vehicle suspensions, tires, cars/vans and eventually two-wheelers. Include unseen combinations rather than only independently seen phones and vehicles. Measure thermal drift, charging interference, vibration, road grade and phone cases. Maintain a supported-device policy and an explicit unsupported state. A few successful local drives do not establish fleet-wide behavior.

**Mount changes and long outages:** detect shifts, slips and drops, invalidate alignment, reduce confidence and reacquire an observable reference before restoring full claims. Pure yaw changes cannot generally be resolved from gravity alone. During multi-hour GNSS loss, error can grow beyond practical usefulness; the system must stop representing a precise fix once its tested envelope is exceeded. Road matching may preserve cross-track plausibility while longitudinal position remains wrong. Optional additional reference sources require a separate product decision.

**GNSS integrity:** distinguish transient outliers, sustained multipath, stale fixes and gradual/spoofed movement. Add checks across position, speed, time, motion and satellite observations, then evaluate false positives as well as missed detections. Innovation gating and C/N₀ trends do not establish spoof resistance. Use replay/injected measurements for controlled fault testing; robust recovery is as important as rejection.

**Energy and thermal operation:** profile the SDK inside the partner app during realistic shifts, with screen on/off, maps active, charging, and warm conditions. Measure incremental energy against the same host app without NavDR. Tune batching and inference duty cycle only where it does not invalidate timing and accuracy assumptions. Track model runtime, CPU load, memory, wake time, dropped samples and output age together. A fast model can still live inside an energy-expensive app.

**Security/privacy:** keep raw location/IMU processing on device by default, minimize retained data, make diagnostic export explicit, encrypt stored/uploaded data where applicable, use access controls and deletion workflows, and separate operational consent from optional model-training use. Trace map-tile, crash-report and telemetry network flows; local inference alone is not proof that location never leaves the phone. Threat-model malformed recordings/maps, bridge access if hybrid, exposed components, tampered models and unauthorized logging. Have an independent security reviewer test the release candidate.

Before a real pilot, obtain jurisdiction-specific legal review of notice/consent or other applicable basis, customer/vendor responsibilities, retention, employee-location monitoring, incident response, training-data rights and deletion obligations. India's DPDP implementation has a published enforcement timeline; verify applicable provisions at the actual launch date rather than declaring blanket compliance from a checklist. [MeitY rules and enforcement timeline](https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa?pageTitle=Digit)

**Maps and dependencies:** manage offline assets, attribution, update/version compatibility, licensing and coverage. Public OSM tiles are not an unrestricted offline-download or production service; use an appropriate licensed/self-hosted offline asset path. [OSM tile usage policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/) Review dataset, model, borrowed filter code, mapping and runtime licenses individually before redistribution.

**QA and releases:** establish code review, unit and numerical tests, native instrumentation tests, deterministic replay regression, fault injection, device/OS compatibility, field acceptance and multi-hour soak tests. Keep an immutable final evaluation set and a separate development corpus. Changes to normalization, acquisition rate, output policy, map matching or model weights can all require accuracy revalidation. Use staged rollout, rollback, incident ownership, and post-release device/OS regression checks. A one-off demo test is insufficient.

### 3.7 Conditions for a standalone company

NavDR could become a company only if the following become true together:

- It provides reproducible, material incremental value over a customer's available location/map stack in a clearly identified operating envelope.
- Buyers pay for that value, renew, and can integrate without a bespoke research project each time.
- The margin after map/runtime services, hosting if any, support, field testing and integration is positive, and customer concentration is manageable.
- The team owns or licenses the required code, data, models and commercial rights, including team/university IP arrangements.
- The team can sustain release engineering, device compatibility, security and customer support after the competition and academic schedule change.
- A defensible asset develops: permissioned domain data, validated generalization, integration quality and accumulated failure/regression knowledge. A small TCN plus EKF/HMM architecture alone is not a commercial moat.

Use a paid pilot and a device-month or fleet-volume license with a minimum commitment as pricing hypotheses, not validated market rates. Establish price from measured customer value and willingness to pay. Calculate break-even customers as monthly fixed operating cost divided by contribution per customer, including amortized integration/support. Do not call projected revenue evidence.

**Stop or narrow the commercial effort** if qualified buyers report little recurring pain, existing providers solve it cheaply enough, the SDK only wins against a weak baseline, mount requirements conflict with actual driver behavior, absolute errors remain unacceptable, or integration/support costs consume the value. A useful SIH research prototype remains a legitimate outcome even if there is no viable standalone business.

Six skilled full-time people can plausibly build the bounded Android prototype, establish credible real-data evidence, and pursue one paid pilot. Universal mounting invariance, robust multi-hour phone-only positioning, spoof-resistant navigation, nationwide map operations and safety-critical deployment remain longer-horizon research/product commitments. More time alone does not guarantee them.
